How commercial losses differ from a house

Commercial buildings are larger, more varied and more connected than a home. Water that starts on an upper floor can travel through a ceiling grid, down a wall and under a shared floor into another suite. Slab floors, carpet tile, drop ceilings and metal studs behave differently from residential finishes, and each needs its own drying approach.

There is also more than one party involved. An owner, a property manager and a tenant may each hold part of the responsibility, and each may have a separate insurance policy. Clear photos and a written list of who was told, and when, make the claim far easier to sort out later.

First-hour safety steps for a commercial water loss

  1. Clear people from any area with a sagging ceiling or standing water.
  2. Shut off the water supply or the fire protection zone valve if you know where it is.
  3. Turn off power to wet areas at the panel only if the floor around it is dry.
  4. Lift electronics, files and stock off the floor and cover what you cannot move.
  5. Photograph every room and the source, and note the time you found it.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and the building manager, and tell us the size of the space and the floor covering.

Fire protection systems

Do not close or work on a fire sprinkler valve unless you know the building procedure. A closed valve may need to be reported and the system restored. Ask the building manager first when you can reach one.

How we handle commercial water damage

Extraction comes first, using pumps and wet vacuums sized to the space. Moisture meters and a thermal camera show how far the water traveled, including above ceilings and behind base. Wet ceiling tile, insulation and drywall that cannot dry are removed. Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed to match the layout, and readings are logged until they match a dry reference.

We reference ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Related steps are on the emergency water removal page and the water mitigation page, and long-term repair is on the water damage repair page.

Commercial water damage and insurance

A commercial property policy, a tenant policy and a landlord policy can each respond to part of the same loss. Sudden damage such as a burst pipe or a failed sprinkler is treated differently from slow seepage. The California Department of Insurance notes that flood is generally excluded from standard property policies. Read the lease, photograph everything and ask each insurer what applies.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not leave stock or equipment on wet floors.
  • Do not run building power in a wet area without checking it.
  • Do not wait for the next business day to start drying.
  • Know where the water main and zone valves are, and label them.
  • Inspect roof drains and gutters before the wet season.
  • Keep a written contact list for the property manager and your insurer.
