When floodwater is an emergency

Floodwater is an emergency from the first inch. It rises quickly in garages, ground-floor rooms and below-grade spaces, and it carries mud and bacteria into carpet, drywall and insulation. The longer it stays, the more porous material has to be thrown away instead of cleaned.

Call the emergency line at any hour. Tell us how deep the water is, where it entered and whether anything electrical or any fuel storage is near it. We use those answers to decide what equipment and protective gear to bring.

The Goleta Valley drains toward the Goleta Slough, and creeks such as Atascadero Creek and Carneros Creek flow toward it. During heavy storms, homes near drainage channels, garages at street level and doors that open onto sloped yards take water first.

Insurance is where flood losses surprise people. The California Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow, and that a new National Flood Insurance Program policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase. Check your policy and the FEMA flood map for your address before the next storm.

Basement and below-grade flooding cleanup

Whether your Goleta home has a full basement, a below-grade garage or a lower-level room, water that enters it usually has nowhere to drain. Pumps remove the water in stages so that walls and slabs are not stressed, and then the space is cleaned and dried.

Porous materials that touched floodwater, such as carpet, pad, insulation and the lower part of drywall, are normally removed. Concrete and framing can be cleaned and dried. A crew confirms with moisture readings before anything is closed up again.

If water keeps entering after the storm has passed, that is a drainage or waterproofing question. Restoration is not waterproofing, and we will say so and point you to a contractor who deals with grading, drains or foundation work.

Sewage backup cleanup: safety first

If sewage has backed up into the house

Raw sewage is Category 3 water. Keep everyone out of the area, do not run water or flush toilets, and do not try to clean it with household tools. Call the 24/7 line and tell us what came up and from which drain.

To find out whether a blockage is in your lateral or in the public line, check with your sanitary district. The Goleta Sanitary District website has an interactive map that locates a lateral connection by street address.

Cleanup follows the S500 approach for contaminated water. The area is contained, standing sewage is extracted, porous materials are removed, hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, and the structure is dried. Because contaminated water is involved, this is not a job to start with a mop and a bucket.

Flood damage restoration questions

Do you handle flood emergencies at night or on weekends in Goleta?

Yes. The emergency line is open 24 hours a day, every day, and same-day response is offered when available. Call once you are safe and tell us how the water entered.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

The California Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood. Some sudden losses are covered, others are not, so read your policy and ask your insurer or agent before you assume anything.

Can the carpet be saved after floodwater?

Carpet and pad that touched floodwater or sewage are normally removed because they cannot be reliably cleaned. Clean-water losses caught quickly sometimes allow carpet to be dried in place, and the crew decides after readings.

Is it safe to stay in the house after a flood?

That depends on the water and the damage. Sewage or contaminated floodwater means keeping out of the affected area at least. If the electrical system was in the water, have it checked before you use it.

Do you provide waterproofing or drainage work?

No. Restoration deals with water that is already in the building. If water keeps entering, ask a drainage or foundation contractor to look at grading and drains.
