A leaking water heater can empty a tank onto the floor, and a failed fitting can keep a steady flow running for hours. Most water heaters sit in a garage, a closet or a utility room, so the leak often reaches subflooring and walls before anyone notices. We remove the water, dry the structure and clean up the area. A plumber repairs or replaces the heater itself, and the two jobs are best done together so the leak is stopped while the drying begins.

Why water heaters leak

Tanks corrode from the inside over time, and a rusted tank seam leaks without warning. Other common causes include a failed temperature and pressure relief valve, a loose drain valve, a worn supply connection and a drain pan that has overflowed. Sediment and age shorten the life of the tank, and a leak from the tank body means replacement.

California law requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement, according to Health and Safety Code section 19211. A heater that has shifted or a strap that has corroded can strain the connections, so check both when you inspect a leak.

First-hour safety steps for a water heater leak

  1. Turn off the power at the breaker for an electric heater, or set a gas heater to pilot or off.
  2. Close the cold water supply valve on top of the heater.
  3. Do not touch the heater or wiring while standing in water.
  4. Open a hot water tap to drain the tank if the plumber advises it.
  5. Photograph the leak, the heater label and the wet area.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and tell us which rooms the water reached.

Gas and electricity near water

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility. Never work near wet wiring, and do not relight a pilot in a wet area.

How we clean up a water heater leak

Extractors take standing water off the floor, and moisture meters find water that traveled into the wall or the subfloor. Wet drywall, baseboards and flooring are removed where they cannot dry in place. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the area, and readings are logged until they match a dry reference.

We reference ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Water from a heater that has been leaking for days can become contaminated, which changes the plan. The related steps are on the water damage cleanup page, and drying is covered on the water mitigation page.

Water heater leaks and insurance

A sudden failure that floods a room is often covered under a homeowners policy for the resulting water damage. The heater itself, and damage from a slow leak that went on for months, are often treated differently. Photograph the label, keep the invoice for the replacement and ask your insurer before work is signed off.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not keep running the heater after a leak starts.
  • Do not leave wet drywall behind a closet wall.
  • Flush the tank and check the anode rod on a schedule your plumber recommends.
  • Install a drain pan with a drain line where the heater sits over finished floors.
  • Look at the base of the heater every few months.
  • Replace an old tank before it fails.
