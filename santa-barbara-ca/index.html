When water is spreading in Santa Barbara

Water spreads faster than most people expect. A failed supply line on an upper floor can send water through a ceiling and down a wall cavity into the unit below within the hour, and a roof leak during a storm can saturate insulation long before a stain shows. The emergency line is for that moment, not for the next business day.

Call, say where the water started and which rooms are wet, and we explain what to shut off and what to leave alone. Then we plan the first visit, extraction and drying included. The cost factors are the same here as everywhere: water category, affected area, materials, drying days and rebuild scope, and we explain scope and pricing before work starts.

Whatever the address, the drying and removal steps follow ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage and S520 for mold. Ask any company whether its crews work to them.

Mission Creek, the January 2023 storm and flood exposure downtown

The City of Santa Barbara says the Lower Mission Creek Flood Control Project covers about 1.3 miles, from Canon Perdido Street to Cabrillo Boulevard, through the West Downtown and Waterfront neighborhoods. After the project, the channel carries about 3,400 cubic feet per second, roughly a 20-year runoff event. Before it, parts of lower Mission Creek handled only 1,500, about an 8-year event.

The city lists notable floods in that watershed in 1914, 1978, 1980, 1995, 1998 and 2005, and says the January 9, 2023 flood was about a 20-year event. Santa Barbara Airport reported its airfield was flooded and closed for approximately 27 hours, and the mayor said the storm produced 400 calls for rescue. The city also says many structures sit within FEMA Special Flood Hazard Areas.

For a homeowner, the practical point is insurance. The California Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, and a new National Flood Insurance Program policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase. Check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center now, not after the next storm.

Santa Barbara neighborhoods and how water moves through them

The Mesa runs about 2.5 miles from Santa Barbara City College to Arroyo Burro Beach, and the Riviera is the hillside above downtown served by Alameda Padre Serra. Homes on hillsides and slopes can take runoff at lower-level doors and garages during storms, so when you call, say whether the water entered from outside or from a pipe.

State Street divides the Eastside from the Westside, and ZIP 93105 includes neighborhoods such as Samarkand, San Roque, Hidden Valley and Upper East. ZIP 93110 covers areas including Hope Ranch. Next door, Montecito (ZIP 93108) and Carpinteria (ZIP 93013) are also within our coverage, although we do not run separate pages for them.

Permits and utilities in the city of Santa Barbara

Permit questions in the city go to the Building and Safety Counter at 630 Garden Street, phone (805) 564-5485 extension 4371. The city says a building permit is generally required to construct, alter or repair a building, with a list of minor exemptions, so ask before a rebuild starts.

For water service problems the City of Santa Barbara Water Resources division takes reports through an online Report an Issue form, and City Hall is at (805) 963-0611. If a public main is the cause, tell the utility in addition to calling us.

If water is still spreading, do this first

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff. For a roof leak, set out buckets and move belongings.

Check the power

If water is near outlets or the panel, switch off a breaker only from a dry floor. Otherwise call the utility or 911 first.

Lift valuables

Raise furniture legs onto blocks and move electronics and papers to a dry room.

Keep everyone out of dirty water

Toilet, sewer and storm water can be contaminated, so keep children and pets away.

Safety checklist before you touch it

  • Turn off power to any wet area before you work near outlets or fixtures.
  • Wear gloves, goggles and a mask rated for fine particles.
  • Do not sweep, brush dry or blow a fan across a large patch.
  • Never mix cleaning products.
  • Stop and call a crew if the area is large, inside a wall or follows flood or sewage water.
  • Questions about symptoms or sensitivity belong with a physician.
