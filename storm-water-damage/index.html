How storms damage Goleta buildings

Wind driven rain finds the weak points. Window and door seals, roof flashing, skylights and chimneys let water in, and clogged gutters push water behind fascia and into the wall. Water that collects against a foundation can seep through a slab joint or enter a crawl space. After a storm, damage may not show until the next day.

Recent storms show how quickly conditions change. The National Weather Service issued a flash flood warning for the Santa Barbara South Coast on January 3, 2026, and Noozhawk reported more than 1.25 inches of rain in an hour in Goleta, with flooding at Fairview and Hollister avenues in Old Town. NASA Earth Observatory recorded over 16 inches in two days in parts of Santa Barbara and Ventura counties during the storms of January 2023.

First-hour safety steps during and after a storm

  1. Stay inside and away from windows, and avoid downed power lines and standing water outside.
  2. If water is coming through a light fixture or outlet, turn off that circuit at the breaker.
  3. Place buckets under drips and move furniture and electronics away from wet walls.
  4. Do not climb on a wet roof to look for the leak.
  5. Photograph the damage from inside and, when safe, from the ground.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and tell us where the water entered.

Never enter standing water with power on

Water in a garage, crawl space or ground floor may touch wiring. Do not wade into it until the power is off and a professional has checked the area.

How we clean up after a storm

We extract water, then use moisture meters and a thermal camera to trace it through ceilings, walls and floors, since rain often travels well beyond where it came in. Wet insulation and drywall that cannot dry are removed. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and we advise on temporary protection for the opening while a roofer or window installer makes the permanent repair.

We reference ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. For a single leak from the roof, see roof leak water damage, and for the drying process in detail, see water mitigation.

Storm damage and insurance

The California Department of Insurance notes that standard homeowners policies exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow, and that a new National Flood Insurance Program policy takes effect 30 days after purchase. Rain coming through a damaged roof is treated differently from surface water entering at the ground. Photograph the entry point, the date and the damage, and ask your insurer which applies.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not wait for the sky to clear before calling.
  • Do not climb onto a wet roof.
  • Do not run the electrical panel with water at the base.
  • Clean gutters and downspouts before each wet season.
  • Check roof flashing and window seals each autumn.
  • Grade soil away from the foundation and extend downspouts.

Local note: storms and drainage in Goleta

The City of Goleta rebuilt the San Jose Creek channel in Old Town and raised its design capacity from a 25-year to a 100-year storm, with a ribbon cutting in May 2014. FEMA flood maps were to be redrawn for about 200 parcels as a result.

The Goleta Valley drains toward the Goleta Slough, fed by Atascadero and Carneros creeks, so water from the hills reaches low ground quickly in heavy rain.

The Goleta Water District describes a semi-arid valley with alternating drought and heavy rain, which is why dry ground can shed water fast at the start of a storm.

Is storm damage the same as flood damage?

No. Rain entering through a roof or window differs from rising surface water, and insurers treat them differently.
