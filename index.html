Water damage restoration is the work of stopping a water source, removing standing water, drying the structure, cleaning and sanitizing what can stay, and rebuilding what cannot. Before we arrive

If water is spreading, do these six things first

You do not need to wait for a crew to start limiting the damage. These steps are safe for most households, and each one shortens the drying job.

Stop the source

Close the shutoff valve at the fixture or the main water valve for the house. For a roof or window leak, move belongings away and set out buckets or a tarp.

Respect the electricity

If water reaches outlets, a panel or a light fixture, stay out of the wet area. Switch off a breaker only if you can reach the panel standing on a dry floor, otherwise call the utility or 911 first.

Lift what you can

Raise small furniture onto blocks, pull rugs and electronics out of the room, and move wet paper and clothing to a dry spot.

Keep people and pets out

Water from a toilet, a sewer line or storm runoff can be contaminated. Keep children and animals away until it is cleaned up.

Photograph first

Take photos of the source, the water line on the walls and damaged items before anything is moved. Your insurer and your crew will both use them.

Local conditions

Water problems that are specific to Goleta

Goleta sits in the Goleta Valley, where the creeks drain toward the Goleta Slough. Atascadero Creek and Carneros Creek are two of the larger tributaries, and the slough sits alongside Santa Barbara Airport and Goleta Beach. If your home is near one of these channels, heavy winter rain can push water toward ground-floor rooms and garages, so check the FEMA flood map for your address before assuming storm water is covered or excluded.

In Old Town Goleta, the City of Goleta rebuilt the San Jose Creek channel and raised its design capacity from a 25-year to a 100-year storm, with a ribbon cutting in May 2014. The city said FEMA maps were expected to be redrawn after the bridge phase to remove about 200 parcels from the flood plain. Maps change slowly, so confirm your own parcel on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center instead of relying on a neighbor.

Roughly half of Goleta households own their home. The Census Bureau puts the owner-occupied rate at 50.7 percent for 2020 to 2024, which means a large share of leaks happen in rentals. A renter who finds water should tell the landlord or property manager in writing the same day, because the owner often decides who dries the unit. We can explain the scope to whichever party is paying.

The same Census figures list a median owner-occupied home value of $1,062,100. At that level, finishes matter, so photographs of the room before the damage help a repair crew match flooring, paint and trim.

Know before you call

Water categories and what they mean for the work

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes three categories of water, and the category decides how much has to be removed and what protective gear the crew wears. Time matters because clean water that sits can move into a worse category.

Water categories.
CategoryTypical sourceWhat it means for the work
Category 1Broken supply line, faucet, tub overflow, clean rainwaterCleanest at the start. Fast extraction and drying usually let most materials stay.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow without solidsContaminated. Carpet pad and soaked porous materials are usually removed, and surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.
Category 3Sewage backup, rising floodwater, water that has stood for daysGrossly contaminated. Porous materials come out, the area is contained, and the crew wears protective equipment.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water emergencies at night or on weekends in Goleta?

Yes. The emergency line is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year, and same-day response is offered when available. Call as soon as you have shut off the water and made the area safe, and be ready to describe where the water is coming from and how far it has spread.

How does pricing work for water damage restoration?

Price depends on the water category, the square footage affected, the materials involved, how many days of drying the structure needs and how much rebuilding follows. We explain the scope of work and how pricing is set before work starts, and we do not quote a number before someone has seen or heard what is wet.

How long does a wet house take to dry?

Drying time depends on what is wet and how much. EPA mold guidance says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, which is why equipment is set up on the first visit. Air movers push dry air across surfaces, dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air, and moisture meters tell the crew when a wall or subfloor has actually reached a dry reading.

Is water damage restoration the same as waterproofing?

No. Restoration deals with water that has already entered the building. Waterproofing and foundation work stop water from getting in at all, and that is a different trade. If water keeps coming back through a wall or slab, we will say so plainly and point you to a contractor who handles drainage or foundation problems.

Do I need flood insurance in Goleta?

The California Department of Insurance states that standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage, and that a new National Flood Insurance Program policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase. Check your own policy, ask your insurer or agent, and look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center before the next storm.

Will you help with my insurance claim?

We document the loss with photos, moisture readings and drying logs, and we explain the scope of work in plain terms so your insurer has a clear record. Coverage and payment decisions belong to your insurer. If you are a renter, tell your landlord or property manager about the loss in writing the same day.
