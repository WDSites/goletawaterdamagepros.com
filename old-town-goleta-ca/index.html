Where Old Town sits

The City of Goleta describes an Old Town project area bounded by Hollister Avenue on the north, State Route 217 on the east, Fairview Avenue on the west and the Goleta Slough to the south and southwest. That comes from a city infrastructure notice and is not a formal neighborhood boundary, so treat it as approximate.

We found no published count of homes and no housing survey for Old Town. For that reason this page makes no claims about building age, basements, crawl spaces or plumbing materials. The damage you are dealing with is described by what you see, not by the name of the district.

What the record shows about water in Old Town

San Jose Creek runs through Old Town. The City of Goleta rebuilt the channel to raise its design storm standard from a 25-year to a 100-year event, with a ribbon cutting in May 2014, according to the city project page. The city also said FEMA maps were expected to be redrawn to remove about 200 parcels from the floodplain after a second phase replaced the Hollister Avenue bridge.

We could not confirm the current map status. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, because a zone label applies to a parcel and not to a whole district.

A local history website describes the land west of Fairview Avenue as mostly farmland because of frequent flooding, part of the slough once called La Patera. It also says a 1928 subdivision, the Fairfield Tract, was planned on swampy land beside San Jose Creek, which flooded regularly. That is history, and it does not describe any home today.

On January 3, 2026, local station KEYT reported that the intersection of Hollister and Fairview avenues flooded until it looked like a lake, with witnesses blaming a clogged storm drain. Mud was left in Old Town parking lots. The station put that weekend at about two inches of rain, after nearly 12 inches over 14 days across Santa Barbara County.

Those reports describe streets and parking lots. They do not say that homes or shops took water, so we do not say it either.

First-hour safety steps in Old Town

  1. Stay out of standing water that reaches outlets, cords or a panel, and keep children and pets out.
  2. Close the fixture valve or the main water valve if a pipe is the source and you can reach it safely.
  3. Switch off a breaker only if you can reach it while standing on dry floor, and never while standing in water.
  4. Move belongings off wet floors where it is safe to do so.
  5. Photograph the source, the water line and damaged items before anything is moved.
  6. Call (805) 291-8940 and describe the rooms, the source and how long the water has been there.

Street water and drain water

Water that came from the street, a storm drain or a sewer line can carry contamination. Do not wade through it, and do not use household fans or an ordinary vacuum on it.

How extraction and drying work

A restoration crew removes standing water with pumps and wet vacuums, then checks the structure with moisture meters and, where useful, a thermal camera. Air movers and dehumidifiers are set up to match the rooms, and readings are repeated until the framing, subfloor and wall bases reach a dry reference. Materials that cannot dry in place, such as soaked insulation or pad, are taken out.

Industry practice is set out in ANSI/IICRC S500, a published standard for water damage restoration. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. Not every material can be saved, and a crew should tell you which ones cannot before anything is discarded. The steps are described further on the emergency water removal page and the water mitigation page.

If drain water is the problem

The Goleta Sanitary District says the property owner maintains the sewer lateral and that the district handles problems in its main. If water backs up through a floor drain, stop using water and find out which side the blockage is on before cleanup is planned. Cleanup of sewage is described on the sewage backup cleanup page.

Insurance documentation

Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and the terms of your policy, so ask your insurer. The California Department of Insurance says standard homeowners policies exclude flood, and that a new National Flood Insurance Program policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the damage, and none of them decides whether a claim is paid.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting to see whether a wet wall dries on its own.
  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them.
  • Walking through street water that may be contaminated.
  • Assuming a flood zone label on a nearby street applies to your building.
  • Painting over a ceiling stain before the source is found.
