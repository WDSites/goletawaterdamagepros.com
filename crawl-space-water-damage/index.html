How water gets into a crawl space

Water arrives from several directions. Rain and surface runoff flow toward the foundation when grading, gutters or downspouts send water the wrong way. Plumbing in the space can leak. Groundwater can rise in a wet season. Vents and openings can let in storm water, and condensation can form on cold pipes and ducts.

The Goleta Valley drains toward the Goleta Slough, and the City of Goleta rebuilt the San Jose Creek channel in Old Town to carry a 100-year storm. Those are reminders that water here moves downhill toward low ground, and a raised house on low ground can see it.

First-hour safety steps for a flooded crawl space

  1. Do not crawl into standing water. Wiring, ducts and debris can hide under it.
  2. Turn off power to the crawl space circuits at the breaker if you can reach it safely.
  3. Shut off the water at the main if a pipe is leaking.
  4. Look from the access hatch with a flashlight and photograph what you see.
  5. Move stored items away from the opening and off damp ground.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and tell us how deep the water is and whether a pipe is running.

Tight spaces and live wiring

A crawl space is a confined space. Water, wiring and low clearance make it unsafe to enter alone. A crew has lighting, protective gear and a way to enter and exit safely.

How we clean up crawl space water damage

Pumps remove standing water, and the crew checks wood framing, the subfloor and the soil with moisture meters. Wet insulation is removed. Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the space, and readings are logged until the framing matches a dry reference. Vapor barriers that have shifted or torn are noted for repair.

We reference ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Water that comes from outside or a drain is treated as contaminated, which changes the removal plan. See flood damage restoration for how those losses are handled.

Crawl space water and insurance

Standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood and surface water, according to the California Department of Insurance, while a burst pipe in the crawl space is often covered as sudden water damage. Which one you have decides the claim, so photograph the water, its source and the date, and ask your insurer.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not go under the house with standing water.
  • Do not leave wet insulation hanging against the subfloor.
  • Extend downspouts away from the foundation.
  • Check the soil grading near the house.
  • Inspect the crawl space before and after the wet season.
  • Keep vents clear and the vapor barrier intact.
