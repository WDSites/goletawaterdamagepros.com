A slab leak is a leak in a pipe that runs beneath or inside a concrete foundation slab. You cannot see it, and it can run for a long time before it shows as a warm spot, a wet floor or a high water bill. If you need emergency slab leak water damage cleanup in Goleta, CA, call our 24/7 line and follow the safety steps below.

We handle the water damage: extraction, drying and removal of wet flooring and baseboards. A leak detection company or plumber locates and repairs the pipe, which sometimes means opening the slab or rerouting the line. The two jobs are separate and both are needed.

How slab leaks happen and how common they are here

Homes built on a slab often carry hot and cold supply lines in or under the concrete. Copper pipes can corrode, rub against rock or concrete as the slab shifts and fail at a joint. We cannot give a local count of slab leaks. What we can say is that slab-on-grade construction is common in coastal California, so the problem is a real one, though it is not the usual source of water damage.

Because the pipe is buried, the leak often shows indirectly. Flooring above lifts, carpet stays damp, a section of floor feels warm, or the sound of running water continues with everything shut off. By then water may have run into the slab, the walls and the subfloor for weeks.

First-hour safety steps for a slab leak

  1. Close the main water shutoff valve to stop the flow.
  2. Check that the floor around any wet area is safe to stand on, and keep electrical cords off it.
  3. Turn off power to wet rooms at the breaker if you can reach it on dry floor.
  4. Lift rugs and furniture off the damp area.
  5. Photograph the wet spot, the meter reading and any warm area.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and a plumber, and tell us how large the wet area is.

A warm floor can mean a hot water line

Water from a hot line can burn. Keep children and pets off a warm, wet spot and do not walk barefoot through it.

How we handle slab leak water damage

We use moisture meters and a thermal camera to map the wet area in flooring, walls and the base of cabinets. Standing water is extracted, and damaged carpet, pad, baseboards and flooring come out where they cannot dry. Concrete holds moisture for a long time, so drying uses dehumidifiers and air movers with readings logged until the slab and surrounding materials are dry.

We reference ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. The process links with emergency water removal when water is standing and with water damage repair after drying.

Slab leaks and insurance

Coverage depends on how the policy treats the pipe, the access to it and the resulting damage. Many policies cover the sudden water damage but not the cost of reaching the pipe, and long-term seepage is often excluded. Ask your insurer what applies before a plumber opens the slab.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not ignore a water bill that jumps without a reason.
  • Do not lay new flooring over a damp slab.
  • Do not jackhammer a slab before the leak has been located.
  • Learn how to read your water meter and test it twice a year.
  • Know where the main shutoff is.
  • Ask a plumber about pressure and pipe condition in older copper systems.
